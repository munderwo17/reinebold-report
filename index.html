WEEK 1  |  The Recap Nobody Asked For By Jeff Reinebold, Special Teams Coordinator of the Heart

Well folks, pull up a folding chair and let me tell you something — Week 1 is like the first day of training camp in the rain. Everybody thinks they're a champion until the whistle blows and somebody's tight end lays an egg. We had fireworks, we had faceplants, and we had a man leave 51 points on his bench like loose change in a gas station parking lot. Grab a coffee. Reinebold's got the tape.

01The Games

Questionable or Kicked Liam Towers • 1-0
defeats
Bang average 161.66 – 135.46

Game of the Week, and it wasn't close. "Questionable or Kicked" dropped a league-high 161.66 and honestly the name is false advertising — nothing questionable about it. The backfield of Ashton Jeanty (32.7) and D'Andre Swift (32.4) ran like they owed somebody money, and Jaxon Smith-Njigba (26.2) was open all afternoon.

Bang average lived up to the billing — 135 is a fine score most weeks, and Kenneth Walker III (34.1) was an absolute bell cow. But Jaylen Waddle (1.2) and George Kittle (3.2) showed up wearing invisibility cloaks.

You averaged 135 and still lost by 26. That's not "bang average," son — that's "bring a bigger gun." Waddle put up 1.2 points; I've seen more production out of the Gatorade table.
Mauled by the Panthers 1-0
defeats
Kelce's Wildest Dream Team 135.26 – 118.1

Lamar Jackson (24.96) and Amon-Ra St. Brown (28.7) did the heavy lifting for a squad whose name suggests they've suffered enough. Chase Brown and Javonte Williams chipped in a combined 43 — that's a balanced diet, folks.

Kelce's Dream Team got a monster from Jahmyr Gibbs (33.6) and Chris Olave (28.2), but the dream turned into a fever nightmare everywhere else. Matthew Stafford (4.1) at QB and a 0.0 from tight end Colston Loveland — you named the team after Kelce and then didn't even start a productive tight end. The irony writes itself.

You built a "Dream Team" and forgot the part where the players score points. Two guys go for 60-plus and the rest of your roster showed up like it was a bye week. That's not a dream, that's a nap.
AllInTheTush 1-0
defeats
Champ 🏆 139.82 – 130.76

The heavyweight fight of the week. "AllInTheTush" got a balanced beatdown led by Bijan Robinson (31.3), Zay Flowers (26.0), and a clean Jalen Hurts (24.72). No single hero — just nobody under 5 in the lineup. That's how you win a slugfest.

"Champ" nearly backed up the name on the strength of Josh Allen's absurd 35.66 and Justin Jefferson (31.2) — 67 points from two guys! — but the supporting cast folded. J.K. Dobbins (3.6) and a 2.0 from kicker Brandon Aubrey left just enough on the table.

Hey Champ — putting the trophy in your name doesn't win the trophy. You got 67 from two studs and still couldn't close. In this league we call that "all hat, no cattle." Adjust the roster, keep the emoji.
Sun God Eclipsed 1-0
defeats
Cracked Out Uncle 156.1 – 103.96

"Sun God Eclipsed" absolutely eclipsed the field with 156.1, riding a shocking breakout from Christian Watson (32.7), David Montgomery (28.9) out of the FLEX, and Trevor Lawrence (26.1). When your third and fourth options go nuclear, the light's out for everybody else.

And now, the tragedy of the week. Ben Lawton's "Cracked Out Uncle" scored 103.96 — while leaving Isaiah Likely (27.8) and Chuba Hubbard (23.7) STAPLED to the bench. That's 51.5 points of production watching from the couch. Started Chargers D/ST for 0.0 and Cameron Dicker for 2.0 instead. Oh, Uncle.

Uncle, I've coached a lot of special teams and I have never — NEVER — seen a man punt 51 points into his own bench. Likely and Hubbard were begging to play and you left 'em in the garage. Lay off whatever you're cracked out on and set your lineup, pal.
Silence of the CeeDee Lambs Alex Farnell • 1-0
defeats
Major Tuddy 120.82 – 106.6

The lowest-scoring affair of the week, but a W's a W. "Silence of the CeeDee Lambs" got carried by Derrick Henry's monstrous 35.3 and Trey McBride (24.5). Everything else was quiet — appropriately, given the name — with Ja'Marr Chase (3.2) going silent when it mattered.

Major Tuddy got tidy lines from CeeDee Lamb (15.4) and Breece Hall (19.8) but the Texans D/ST posted a MINUS 4.0. Yes, negative. You can't make this up — the defense actively subtracted from the cause.

Major Tuddy, your defense scored negative four. That's not defense, that's a hostage situation. And CeeDee — the Lambs stayed silent, but that mascot of yours cost you the game single-handedly.

05The Last Word

Set your lineups, trust your studs, and for the love of all that is holy, Uncle — start the guys who score.

Reinebold out. See you next week when the tape doesn't lie and neither do I.
